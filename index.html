What's inside: the Taptic Engine

iPhones use a linear resonant actuator (LRA). A small weight with a magnet is held by springs. A coil pushes it side to side when current flows through. The whole phone moves in reaction, and your hand feels it.

Resonance

The mass and springs have a natural frequency, around 150 to 200 Hz in many phones. Driving the coil near that frequency gives the strongest buzz for the least power.

Crisp taps

A very short pulse, then an opposite pulse to stop the mass quickly. This "braking" makes a sharp click instead of a mushy hum.

Software shapes it

iOS Core Haptics lets apps describe events as transient (a tap) or continuous (a rumble), each with intensity and sharpness, and sync them with audio.

Old motor vs. Taptic Engine

Eccentric rotating mass (older phones)Linear resonant actuator (iPhone)

Where you feel it

Games (hits, engines, explosions), keyboard feedback, the Home button on older models, Apple Pay confirmation, scroll pickers, and silent-mode alerts. In each case the haptic confirms something happened without you looking.
